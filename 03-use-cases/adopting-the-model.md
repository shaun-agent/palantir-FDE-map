# Adopting the FDE Model — The Playbook AI Companies Are Copying

Why now: FDE postings grew **>800% Jan–Sep 2025**. OpenAI stood up an FDE org in 2024 (targeting
~50 by end-2025); Anthropic grew its Applied AI group with FDE roles and a manager track; 100+ YC
startups hire FDEs. The shared bet: **AI adoption is bottlenecked at deployment, not model quality** —
which is the exact problem shape the FDE model was built for.

## The seven principles (per the getperspective.ai playbook)

1. **Hire engineer-diplomats** — production engineers with customer-facing range, not sales
   engineers with demo skills.
2. **Embed at customer sites** — single-account assignments, 8–16 week engagements (AI-lab variant).
3. **Deliver immediately** — working prototype in week one, on the customer's own data.
4. **Treat discovery as engineering** — 5–15 customer conversations a week is the job, not overhead.
5. **Build customer-specific ontologies first** — [domain modeling precedes any LLM deployment](../01-core-concepts/ontology-first.md).
6. **Route feedback through FDEs** — a direct, structural channel into product management.
7. **Refuse systems-integration work** — customer-owned applications, never staff augmentation.

## Org-design checklist (vinoo.io + McGrew)

- **Make it structural, not optional.** Palantir's Project Frontline rotation (250+ engineers)
  worked because the CTO owned it. A volunteer program is tourism.
- **Develop, don't just hire.** Rotate product engineers through the field with mentors and lead
  check-ins; align each rotation with the engineer's future product area so they become users
  before builders.
- **Staff both disciplines.** Code + institution ([Delta–Echo](../01-core-concepts/delta-echo-pairing.md)).
  An FDE org with no domain/politics function re-learns Palantir's 2008 lessons at your expense.
- **Pick markets with the no-incumbent test.** Deploy embedded teams where no established
  competitor already has them.
- **Price outcomes, not time.** The startups hitting eight figures in year one priced results;
  T&M pricing starts the [consulting-trap](../02-mental-models/the-consulting-trap.md) clock.
- **Protect the flywheel.** Someone on the product side must own abstracting field patterns
  ([flywheel](../01-core-concepts/productization-flywheel.md)), or deployment cost never declines.
- **Expect cultural friction and longer timelines.** Field and product teams see the world
  differently; integration is deliberate bridge-building, and the economics compound on a
  multi-year horizon (Palantir needed Thiel-grade patience).

## Common mistakes (named in the sources)

- Over-long rotations that strand engineers in the field
- Disconnecting field experience from product work (kills the flywheel *and* the career path)
- Treating the FDE org as a separate consultancy with its own P&L and utilization targets
- Staffing it with "loyalists and craftsmen" instead of "domain rebels and prototypers" (McGrew)
- Assuming customer data is ready (it never is — budget the [ontology work](../01-core-concepts/ontology-first.md))

## Sizing

Playbook heuristic: **~1 FDE per $2–5M of enterprise pipeline.** Comp at the AI labs rivals
staff-level Meta/Google packages ($220–280K + equity in OpenAI's NY postings), with ~99%
applicant rejection — budget accordingly.
